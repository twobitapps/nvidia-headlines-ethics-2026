Nvidia and Jensen Huang: ethics sentiment in WSJ, NYT and The Economist

We scanned 81,387 WSJ, NYT and Economist headlines from 11 May to 11 Nov 2026, every headline on 396 daily archive, sitemap and weekly edition pages. 258 of them are about Nvidia or Jensen Huang. Each was scored for ethics content by three independent raters on a five-point rubric; the charts use the median score.

Ethics coverage turned sharply negative: 6% of headlines before 29 Oct were ethics-negative, against 82% from the midterm election (3 Nov) onward. The break came on 29 Oct, five days before the election, when NYT reported that Huang was the target of a Chinese spy plot. The election does not explain the shift. Then came a ten-day lull with almost no follow-up. The surge began on 8 Nov, when Huang admitted the affair in a letter to staff and Nvidia's board opened a probe.

How much the raters agreed

All three raters gave the same score on 234 of 258 headlines and never differed by more than 1 point. The before and after gap holds for each rater alone: 5% to 7% ethics-negative before 29 Oct, 79% to 82% from 3 Nov.

What this does and doesn't show

  • The three raters are the same model with one rubric, so they share any blind spot in it. High agreement means the scoring is consistent, not that it is correct.
  • Most headlines are plain business news and score 0, so the means mostly track how many scandal headlines there are.
  • The "after" period is almost entirely 8 to 11 Nov, one news cycle about one story. It cannot separate a post-election shift from the scandal itself, which broke on 29 Oct.
  • The ten-day lull has an ordinary explanation in the headlines: on 8 Nov the story changed from Huang as a spy's target to Huang's own conduct and a board probe, and markets reopened the next day. Why the leak came five days before the vote and the letter five days after it is a question this data raises but cannot answer.
  • Ten matches were removed by hand: four false matches (other Jensens, and "envidiar") and six NYT en Español translations of stories already counted.
  • WSJ's daily archive leaves out live-coverage posts, videos and podcasts, so before November those are counted only where searches caught them.
  • The Economist is incomplete: access was interrupted partway through, so its 11 headlines are a floor.
  • The spy and affair claims are as reported by these outlets and were not independently verified.
